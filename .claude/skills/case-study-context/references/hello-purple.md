# Hello Purple — what's actually public

`https://hello.purple.group/` is, as of now, a **pre-launch teaser page with
a waitlist form**. There is no public documentation, no SDK reference, no
CLI docs, nothing publicly describing Purple Engine's surfaces. This isn't a
gap in research — nothing else is out there yet.

That matters for two reasons. First, it means the case study can't be
grounded in hands-on experience of Hello Purple itself; the cTrader MCP is
the substrate instead, which is exactly what Purple's brief asks for.
Second, it confirms the JD's own framing — the product is genuinely still
finding its shape, and this role is meant to give it one.

## What the page actually says

Positioning is aspirational rather than technical: an invitation to become
the architect of your own financial world. The narrative is that Purple has
spent roughly fifteen years building fintech piece by piece, and is now
bringing those pieces together into one platform. The audience is described
broadly as builders — explicitly spanning from company leadership down to
an individual creator, not just professional developers. The call to action
is a form asking what the visitor would build with them.

No pricing, no technical specifics, no launch date.

## Purple Engine, per the job description

What isn't on the public site but is stated in the JD: Purple Engine is the
technical layer beneath Hello Purple — a set of SDKs, CLI, MCP servers, and
auth surfaces. Both Hello Purple and Purple Engine are owned as products by
the person in this role: vision, roadmap, communication, documentation, and
what ultimately ships.

That MCP servers are already named as a first-class Purple Engine surface
is the strongest single argument for shaping this case study's build as an
MCP server rather than a standalone script — it lands in the exact category
of artifact the role is meant to own.
